AI Insights 2026

Powered by AI, design's value is moving from production to direction and/from execution to judgment. AI 2026 Insights maps where this upstream motion is already underway, and what it demands from creatives next.

AI Insights 2026

Understanding AI’s effect on the economy.

View full report

The gap between producing something and knowing why it works is where direction lives, in the act of design.

At this year’s SCAD AI Summit, one gap showed up consistently: the space between those who produce work and those who shape direction. Most classrooms and boardrooms still reward what gets produced, and how fast, but the real work happens earlier.

In 2026, SCADask surveyed 100+ leaders across design, tech, and enterprise to understand how AI impacts knowledge work, design workflows, and what barriers still exist with regard to adoption.

Transformation

Where designers and industries adapt and restructure

Transformation appears in the gap between what designers assumed would stay and what has already given way, like a definition of craft that expanded or an ethical instinct that formed before the framework existed to name it.

63%

of practitioners define their primary KPI for AI success as time saved.

“What AI is most helpful for is the very beginning — when you’re starting just to explore — or at the very end, a polish. But that middle part is actually where people’s voices and your own unique perspective come in.”

— CJ Jones (B.F.A., advertising, 2011), Head of Design for GenAI, Canva

Horizon

Where design moves before the map exists

What surfaced consistently across the summit wasn’t prediction. It was direction — a shared recognition of where value is moving and what the field needs to claim before someone else does.

31%

of respondents identify multi-agent orchestration as the emerging AI trend most likely to shape their industry, leading all categories.

“Unlike humans, who learn through embodied experience, exploration, and gradual growth, AI has no ‘childhood.’ [You] make AI practice, play, and improvise with humans by building AI that learns like a developing partner — not a perfect machine — and opens a huge, unexplored space for teaching, creativity, and skill-building.”

— SCADask survey respondent
