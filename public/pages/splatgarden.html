THE EXPLORATION

What if a portfolio
was a place?

The garden combines procedural plant growth, effects simulation, AI stylization, and vertex animation textures. Visitors can experience the work in context, then explore how each piece was built.

Gaussian splatting transforms photographs into a 3D scene that can be viewed from different perspectives in real time. Here, the technique becomes an accessible, interactive showcase.

BEHIND THE SCENE

How the garden grows.

Asset creation +

Asset creation

Plant Growth Tools

Two custom Houdini tools were built to replicate the organic structure and growth of grape hyacinth and daffodils. The hyacinth tool drives floret placement using the golden angle to recreate natural spiral phyllotaxis, while the daffodil tool layers KineFX rigging with Vellum simulation for soft, organic petal motion.

Graph Hyacinth

Daffodil

Stylized Textures

A custom AI stylization tool was used to extract shapes and structure from original, realistic texture, combining them with the look of a chose style reference. This generated a painterly, art-directed output while preserving the underlying detail. The result was next refined in Houdini's COP Network and Substance Painter.

Landscape Texture

Effects with Gaussian splats in Houdini

Gaussian Splats carry native attributes like alpha, scale, color, and orientation that can be read and manipulated directly in Houdini. Particle and pyro simulations were applied directly to the splat alongside the GSOPs plugin, significantly speeding up the effects workflow.

Final Result

Technical Breakdown

Procedural Vine Growth in Unreal Engine

Two procedural systems drive the vine's growth. The stem runs on a real-time GPU workflow, using UV directional masking and vertex color to control where the mesh grows, with World Position Offset handling the deformation. Layered on top, Unreal's Motion Design plugin scatters flower and leaf assets across the mesh using a Cloner component, with Plane and Sphere Effectors controlling their scale and growth timing. Driven through Sequencer and Blueprint, the blooming follows the vine's direction in real time, creating a fully procedural growth sequence from stem to flower.

Procedural Vine Grow Tool

Environment Assets

The team procedurally modeled the foliage in SpeedTree, optimizing polygon counts throughout to maintain real-time performance. Those assets then moved into Substance Designer, where textures took on the painterly aesthetic of the garden scene.

Daisies

The scene was populated using a combination of Procedural Content Generation (PCG) and Unreal Engine's Foliage Painting Tool, each serving a distinct purpose. PCG handled large-scale distribution across the environment, while the Foliage Painting Tool allowed for hand-crafted placement in areas requiring finer detail and artistic control.

The production pipeline +

01 / Capturing the Scene

To capture the scene, a camera was set up directly within the original Unreal Engine environment and used to render an image sequence from hundreds of different angles covering the full garden from every perspective needed for accurate, highly detailed Gaussian Splat training.

02 / Training Gaussian Splats

The captured image sequence — 990 images spanning the full scene — was trained into a Gaussian Splat using PostShot, resulting in approximately 3 million Gaussians optimized for real-time playback.

03 / Building WebGL

The interactive experience is built through four technical layers.

The 3DGS Foundation

Rendering runs on @sparkjsdev/spark, a Three.js-native splat renderer, alongside Three.js for camera and post-processing, Vite 6 for builds, and OpenUSD for scene data. Assets like gazebo, vines, daffodils, and grape hyacinths were authored in Houdini and Unreal Engine 5, captured through a 990-camera rig, reconstructed via COLMAP, and trained in Postshot and Lichtfeld Studio into a 96 MB .splat file of roughly 3 million Gaussians.

Hand Tracking

MediaPipe handles in-browser gesture recognition, mapping hand movement to scene controls: a single-hand pinch orbits the scene, a quick pinch-tap fires a click effect, and a two-hand spread or contract adjusts zoom.

Interactive Particle and Click FX

GPU shader effects apply spatially to the Gaussian field, triggered by clicks or gestures. Eight effects span generative techniques: FBM-warped dissolves, Physarum-based slime molds, curl-noise vortex drift, chromatic ripples, scan lines, and more.

Post-FX and Cinematic Styling

The rendered frame passes through an EffectComposer for full-screen shader effects, including three painterly modes: Monet's anisotropic Kuwahara filter for a wet-paint look, Matisse's posterized color with bold edges, and Seurat's pointillist dot-grid sampling.
